    The port

    Every other chord display takes your keyboard away from you.

    1→3apps on one controller at once

    Open a chord tool on Windows and it claims the MIDI port for itself. Kontakt goes quiet, or the DAW stops receiving, or the app reports the device as busy, so the thing that was meant to help you play stops you playing.

    Chordlight opens no port exclusively. It listens alongside whatever else is holding the controller: your DAW, your sampler and Chordlight all hear the same keyboard at the same time. All inputs at once if you like. Virtual cables appear like any hardware port, and a keyboard plugged in mid-session shows up without a restart.

    Three readings of every chord

    Not everyone thinks in letters.

    Chord displays name chords in letters. A large part of the keyboard-playing world, worship, gospel, choral and most of the teaching world, works in numbers and in sol-fa. Chordlight reads all three at once, from the same hands. Switch with one click, or show all three in pop-out windows.

    What it is not.

    Being straight about this is what keeps the reviews honest.

    Does it work with my keyboard?
    Any keyboard or controller that sends MIDI over USB or Bluetooth, including digital pianos. Chordlight listens to all inputs by default.
    Does it make sound?
    No. Your keyboard or your DAW does. Chordlight shows and records; it does not synthesise, and it does not include a piano.
    Does it listen to audio to find the chords?
    No. It reads MIDI. The microphone is for your voice-over, not for chord detection.
    What is a .chordlight file?
    A single shareable take: the MIDI, the audio and the lesson title in one file. Anyone with Chordlight opens it and sees the lights and hears the audio together.
    Does it record my screen?
    No. The video is drawn by the app at 1080p, or 4K at Best, so it looks the same whatever your windows are doing. System audio and your microphone are mixed in.
    Can I use it on two computers?
    Yes. One licence key covers two. Deactivate this device in About frees a seat so you can move it yourself.
    Does it need the internet?
    Only to activate and for an occasional check-in. It keeps working offline for thirty days between check-ins. There is no account inside the app, no analytics and no telemetry.
    Is there a plug-in version, or a phone version?
    No. It is a standalone desktop app for Windows and macOS, not a VST3 or an AU, and not on iOS or Android.
    Will it really share any keyboard?
    Almost any. The one exception is a controller whose manufacturer ships an exclusive-access driver, which nothing can share. Route it through loopMIDI in that case and everything downstream works normally.
